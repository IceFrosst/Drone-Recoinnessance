1 · What the simulator is

A browser simulator of passive RF direction-finding from a fixed-wing drone over a contested front. It flies predefined routes, records bursts (frequency, strength, a two-channel cone angle), and after landing builds a probability heat map of where each high-value emitter sits — drone operators (UAS), jammers (EW), command posts (C2) and comms relays. The published artifact is the heat-map simulator (heatmap.html); the repo also holds the live-tasking simulator (index.html).

RF front end — preselect filter + LNA (and "does an LNA cost us?")

Answered with data. A preselect filter sits before the ADC, so it is the only thing that stops an out-of-band jammer overloading the receiver; the LNA is digital-sensitivity only and lowers overload headroom. Calibrated to measured bladeRF noise figure (4.9–15.8 dB across band) and ADI app-note 1836 (a 15 dB LNA: +3.8 dB sensitivity, −15 dB dynamic range).

Real-world threats & new technology (both sides, 2024–26)

  • Jammer waveforms — barrage / swept / pulsed, each with duty & sweep; drives the excision result above.
  • Fibre-optic FPV — emit no RF, so unlocatable at any price. With 30% on fibre the ceiling is ~70% of operators — shown, not hidden (Russia 30–50% / Ukraine ~15% of FPV).
  • Low-rate LoRa/ELRS links (~−120 dBm) — sit below an energy detector; only cyclostationary detection catches them.
  • Decoy emitters — transmit a real control-link signature from empty ground → a false fix no direction-finding removes (identical decoy-fix count across every equipped payload).
  • SEI (specific emitter ID) — a hardware fingerprint re-links an operator after it retunes. Operators located 85%→96% when teams change link settings (E12).
  • GNSS spoofing — a coherent false position (distinct from the existing denial/drift). The sharpest warning: actionable fixes (≤500 m) collapse 69%→14% while the located count and the 95%-ellipse calibration barely move — a tight ellipse is no proof a fix is right, and no RF countermeasure touches it (E13/E28).

5 · Operational insights the sim surfaced

  • Freshness beats quantity. Against a relocating enemy with "keep-all" memory, flying more sorties lowers accuracy (45%→24% operators from 2→8 flights) as stale recordings pile up; fading old data recovers it (→50%), and the confidently-wrong rate drops 22%→1% (E16/E17).
  • Graceful, honest degradation. As the enemy relocates more (0→70%/flight) operator hit-rate falls 65%→38% but the located count holds and the 95% ellipse stays calibrated — the system widens uncertainty instead of going overconfident (E18).
  • Flight plan. Multi-angle > grid > parallel > single pass (operators 53% vs 43%) — crossing headings still resolve the bearing mirror even with the full payload (E19).
  • Receiver is decisive. Even with every countermeasure on, the fused dual-receiver beats bladeRF-alone by +24 pp operators; the array alone underperforms on band coverage (E20).
  • Robust to clutter. Light/typical/heavy background spectrum (80/150/300 emitters) give identical accuracy — the 5-stage filter + deinterleaver cut through density (E11).

8 · Honest limits (shown on purpose)

  • All numbers are modelled, calibrated to published hardware/EW figures (docs/research.md) — not a bench test of this payload. They show the right direction and rough magnitude of each effect.
  • Fibre-optic FPV are unlocatable (hard ceiling); decoys are false fixes nothing removes; GNSS spoofing needs anti-spoof/inertial navigation (out of this payload's scope).
  • Nulling assumes the two channels can be phase-calibrated on a vibrating airframe — the hard part a real build must still prove.
  • Still unmodelled: a field-validated hop fingerprint, 3D geolocation, mutual coupling, weather, and aircraft endurance/survivability.
