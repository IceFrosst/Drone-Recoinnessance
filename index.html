    About the model

    This is a physics-based illustration for briefing and discussion. Every number on screen is a modelled estimate. None of it is real RF data, and none of it is a claim of field performance.

    Bearings are combined by weighted least squares. Each bearing is weighted by its expected cross-range error, its angular error times its range, so strong bearings count more than faint ones. The 95% ellipse comes from the solution covariance, inflated when the bearings disagree more than the model expects. Bearings more than 3σ from the fix are dropped one at a time, worst first, up to a quarter of them, so reflections and steep-zone bearings don't drag the fix away.

    Contacts are classified by signal fingerprint: band, hopping, cadence and bandwidth. Confidence builds burst by burst and grows faster on strong signals. A contact whose bearings never converge is treated as moving.

    Dense spectrum and deinterleaving. The sector carries a configurable background population of friendly, civilian and other-unit emitters on top of the designated targets. Every emission becomes an anonymous burst, and an online clustering algorithm sorts the stream back into tracks by frequency, bandwidth, hop signature and bearing, with co-channel collisions and re-acquisition across silences. It scores itself against ground truth: each target is isolated, merged, split, or lost in the clutter, and the panel reports the tracks formed, the other control links found, and any false control-link tracks. Located fixes are the targets the deinterleaver could isolate; merged and split targets carry a widened, flagged ellipse.

    Five-stage classification pipeline. Every event the receiver hears runs through five gates in order: band, self-noise blanking, noise-floor margin, hop fingerprint, then persistence and geometric convergence. A signal must pass all five to be shown as a located transmitter. The cheap gates run first and discard the most, so the expensive direction-finding only runs on a shrinking candidate set. The simulator proves the logic flows — the right signals survive and clutter dies — but it does not prove the fingerprint separates a real target from real-world clutter. That proof needs a field recording, and the fingerprint values are placeholders until then.

    Frontline degradation (the "Frontline RF" toggle). A real sector adds error a clean model omits. Modelled here: array and aircraft-attitude bearing error (folded into the reported ellipse, so it stays honest); multipath fading that makes signal strength swing second to second; a near-far floor rise when the band is busy, which buries weak emitters; shadowing from our own airframe; and burst mis-association, where a burst is filed under the wrong nearby target and the convergence gate has to catch it. Turn it off to compare against the clean illustration.

    Still not modelled, and where field data would matter most: a validated hop fingerprint (the Stage-4 values are placeholders); 3D geolocation including emitter height; an adversary that changes frequency, bursts briefly, or runs silent; fibre-optic FPV drones (no RF at all); real coherent-array calibration and mutual coupling; weather and ducting; downlink bandwidth, endurance and survivability of the aircraft; and the absence of ground truth in the field (here the error-vs-truth is known only because the simulator placed the emitters).

    The constants sit at the top of the script, so field-test figures can replace them.
